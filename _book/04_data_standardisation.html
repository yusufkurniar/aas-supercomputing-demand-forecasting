5.1 Purpose

The purpose of this step is to standardise the integrated historical High-Performance Computing (HPC) data to support consistent analysis across reporting years.

This stage uses the reconciled annual demand and allocation summary and the Field of Research (FoR) summary produced during Data Integration.

Standardisation focuses on aligning reporting periods, variable names, units, research field classifications, and data quality labels where necessary. Original values, source information, and derivation assumptions are retained to maintain traceability.

Numerical demand and allocation values are not converted at this stage. Compute-unit conversion is addressed separately in the Data Conversion chapter.

5.2 Reporting Period

Reporting periods are standardised using the ending year of each annual reporting period. For example, 2013/14 is represented as 2014, and 2025/26 as 2026.

The analysis covers 13 annual observations from 2014 to 2026. The year column is retained as the analytical year, while source_period preserves the original reporting period.

Both variables are carried forward from Data Integration. This step validates their consistency without modifying the existing year assignments.

Code 
library(dplyr)
library(readxl)

annual_data <- read_excel(
  "assets/data/data clean/reconciled_demand_allocation_summary.xlsx"
)

year_check <- annual_data |>
  mutate(
    expected_year = as.integer(
      sub("^.*[/\\-]", "", source_period)
    ) + 2000L
  ) |>
  filter(is.na(year) | year != expected_year)

5.3 Institution Names

Institution names may differ across historical sources because of abbreviations, naming conventions, or organisational changes.

The reconciled annual demand and allocation summary and Field of Research (FoR) summary do not contain institution-level observations. Therefore, no institution-name mapping is applied to these datasets.

Institution-level standardisation may be undertaken separately if institutional allocation data are included in subsequent analysis.

5.4 Field of Research Classification

Historical Field of Research (FoR) data use different classification systems, including ANZSRC 2008 and ANZSRC 2020.

The reconciled FoR dataset from Data Integration retains the originally reported research fields and classification codes, alongside a standardised FoR category for longitudinal analysis.

Existing mappings are retained, including combined historical categories where a direct one-to-one mapping is not supported. Classification status and standardisation notes document estimated classifications, unresolved mappings, and differences between reporting systems.

This step validates the existing classifications without changing allocation shares, demand estimates, or other numerical values.

5.4.1 Historical FoR Reporting Groups

For visualisation, the FoR data are divided into two periods: 2014–2020 and 2021–2026.

For 2014 - 2020, originally reported research fields are grouped into consistent historical reporting categories. In particular, the separately reported Geosciences, Earth Sciences, and Environmental Sciences categories are combined into Earth & Environmental Sciences. Radioastronomy is combined with Physical Sciences, while Engineering and Technology are combined into Engineering & Technology.

For 2021 - 2026, the existing standardised ANZSRC 2020 FoR categories are retained.

These groupings are used for reporting and visualisation only. Original research field names, allocation shares, estimated compute values, and source information remain unchanged. Differences in data scope, particularly Pawsey-wide supercomputer usage in 2016/17, are retained as comparability limitations.

Code 
# Create FoR reporting categories without modifying source values
for_data <- for_data |>
  mutate(
    for_analysis_group = case_when(
      year <= 2020 &
        research_field_reported %in% c(
          "Geosciences",
          "Earth Sciences (exc. Geosciences)",
          "Environmental Sciences",
          "Earth & Environmental Sciences"
        ) ~ "Earth & Environmental Sciences",

      year <= 2020 &
        research_field_reported %in% c(
          "Radioastronomy",
          "Physical Sciences (exc. radio astronomy)",
          "Physical Sciences"
        ) ~ "Physical Sciences",

      year <= 2020 &
        research_field_reported %in% c(
          "Engineering",
          "Technology",
          "Engineering & Technology"
        ) ~ "Engineering & Technology",

      year <= 2020 &
        research_field_reported %in% c(
          "Other / Unreported",
          "Other / Unclassified"
        ) ~ "Other / Unclassified",

      year <= 2020 ~ research_field_reported,

      TRUE ~ for_standard_2020
    )
  )

5.5 HPC Facilities and Systems

Historical HPC demand and allocation data originate from Australia’s national computing facilities, including the National Computational Infrastructure (NCI) and Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre.

The reconciled annual summary retains facility and system information within the original source descriptions. These include NCI allocation records, Pawsey historical reporting, and more recent NCMAS outcomes identifying Gadi and Setonix CPU and GPU resources separately.

Facility and system information is therefore available for some reporting periods, although it is not consistently represented in dedicated structured variables across the integrated annual dataset.

At this stage, the existing facility and system descriptions are retained without modifying the original records. Where system-level information is available, it will inform resource classification and conversion assumptions in the Data Conversion chapter.

Differences between NCI and Pawsey accounting conventions, system configurations, and CPU/GPU resource measures are preserved as comparability considerations. No assumption is made that allocations from different systems are directly equivalent.

5.6 Resource Type

Historical HPC demand and allocation records include different computing resources, primarily CPU and GPU systems. The availability of resource-level information varies across reporting periods.

Earlier reporting years generally provide aggregate allocation or demand figures without a consistent CPU/GPU breakdown. Some historical systems, including Raijin and Magnus, are identified in the source records, but their system-level allocation shares are not always available.

More detailed resource-level information is available for later years. The 2020/21 allocation records identify Magnus, FlashLite, MASSIVE, and Gadi, while the 2021/22 records distinguish MASSIVE, Gadi, Setonix CPU, and Setonix GPU. From 2022/23 onwards, several reporting years provide separate Gadi CPU, Setonix CPU, and Setonix GPU allocations.

However, the availability of resource-level demand data is more limited. In particular, the 2023/24 and 2024/25 demand estimates are derived from aggregate allocation and fulfilment rates, without a directly reported CPU/GPU demand breakdown.

For standardisation, resource types are classified as CPU, GPU, or Unspecified only where supported by the original source information. Records without sufficient resource-level detail retain an unspecified classification rather than being assigned an estimated CPU/GPU split.

No numerical allocation or demand values are redistributed between resource types at this stage. Resource-specific conversion factors and comparability adjustments are addressed in the Data Conversion chapter.

5.7 Units

The reconciled annual demand and allocation summary contains different computational accounting units across reporting periods.

The 2013/14 records are expressed in kSU. The 2014/15 and 2015/16 records contain mixed units, with demand expressed in million hours and allocation expressed in kSU. The 2016/17 records use million core-hours, while 2017/18 uses million hours and 2018/19 uses million CPU-hours.

From 2019/20 to 2025/26, annual demand and allocation values are recorded in kSU. However, the use of the same unit label does not necessarily establish equivalence between different HPC systems or CPU and GPU resources.

At the standardisation stage, original unit labels are retained without numerical conversion. Mixed-unit records are explicitly identified to prevent inappropriate direct comparisons.

The Data Conversion chapter will assess system-specific conversion factors, distinguish CPU and GPU accounting where supported, and document any assumptions required to establish comparable analytical measures.

5.8 Data Quality and Comparability

The integrated historical HPC dataset combines directly reported demand and allocation figures with values derived from reported demand-to-supply relationships, fulfilment rates, and available computing capacity. Consequently, the quality and comparability of observations vary across reporting years.

5.8.1 Data Provenance and Estimation

Directly reported observations are retained as published in the original sources. Where demand figures are unavailable, existing estimates derived during Data Integration are preserved together with their calculation assumptions and source references.

For example, the 2014/15 and 2015/16 demand estimates are derived from reported demand-to-supply or fulfilment relationships. The 2017/18, 2020/21, 2023/24, and 2024/25 demand estimates similarly rely on reported allocation totals and demand-to-supply or fulfilment information.

The 2016/17 and 2018/19 records include capacity-based information or conservative demand proxies. These are distinguished from directly reported allocation and demand figures.

No demand estimates or allocation values are recalculated during Data Standardisation.

5.8.2 Comparability Across Reporting Years

Historical observations differ in their reporting scope, computational accounting units, and availability of system-level information.

In particular, the 2016/17 data include Pawsey-wide supercomputer usage and capacity information, which are not directly equivalent to NCMAS allocation outcomes in other years.

Differences between CPU and GPU resources, system-specific accounting conventions, and incomplete resource breakdowns also limit direct comparisons across reporting periods.

These limitations are retained in the analysis rather than resolved through unsupported assumptions.

5.8.3 Treatment for Subsequent Analysis

The standardisation process preserves original numerical values, source references, estimation assumptions, and existing data quality information.

Observations with different reporting scopes, derived demand values, or uncertain conversion assumptions will be explicitly considered during Data Conversion, exploratory analysis, and forecasting model evaluation.

Where appropriate, sensitivity analysis will be used to assess how historical proxies and comparability limitations affect the forecasting results.

5.9 Output

The output of this step is a standardised dataset that retains the original reported information while providing consistent classifications for subsequent conversion and analysis.
