8.1 Purpose

This chapter aims to develop transparent, reproducible, and statistically defensible forecasting models to estimate Australian national research supercomputing demand through 2030.

Building on the harmonised historical dataset and initial findings presented in Chapters 03 - 06, the analysis evaluates alternative approaches for modelling annual researcher demand, expressed in CPU-equivalent core-hours.

The modelling framework has four objectives:

  1. Establish a suitable historical demand series for forecasting, accounting for data quality, reporting differences, and conversion assumptions.
  2. Develop and compare candidate forecasting models, prioritising simple and interpretable approaches such as linear and log-linear trends.
  3. Examine the sensitivity of projected demand to the substantial increase observed in 2026, distinguishing potential structural changes from temporary fluctuations.
  4. Generate alternative demand projections for 2027 - 2030 to support national HPC infrastructure planning and policy discussions.

Following expert statistical feedback, particular emphasis is placed on model simplicity, transparent assumptions, and the limitations of extrapolating trends from a relatively short and heterogeneous historical dataset.

The forecasts are intended to provide evidence-informed planning scenarios rather than precise predictions of future researcher demand.

8.2 Forecast Target and Data Suitability

8.2.1 Forecast Target

The forecasting analysis considers three related but distinct measures of Australian national research supercomputing demand:

  1. Overall HPC demand, expressed in thousands of service units (kSU).
  2. CPU demand, expressed in CPU core-hours.
  3. GPU demand, expressed in GPU-hours.

These measures represent harmonised estimates of computing resources requested by researchers, rather than allocated resources or available computing capacity. CPU and GPU demand are modelled separately because their resource units and conversion assumptions are not directly interchangeable.

The overall kSU series provides a reference for aggregate demand, while the CPU and GPU series support resource-specific forecasts relevant to future infrastructure planning.

8.2.2 Historical Data Suitability

The historical demand series provides a consistent analytical unit for forecasting, but it should not be interpreted as a fully homogeneous set of directly observed demand measurements.

Depending on the year and reporting source, historical estimates include directly reported demand, derived estimates, and proxy-based measures. Differences in facility coverage, reporting definitions, and CPU/GPU conversion assumptions introduce uncertainty into comparisons across years.

The relatively small number of annual observations also limits the complexity of forecasting models that can be estimated reliably. Consequently, simple trend-based models will be prioritised, while more flexible time-series approaches will be used as benchmarks.

The substantial increase in reported demand in 2026 presents an additional uncertainty. Available historical observations do not establish whether this increase represents a sustained shift in researcher demand, a temporary fluctuation, or a difference in measurement or reporting scope.

Accordingly, the modelling analysis will consider both the complete historical series (2014–2026) and a sensitivity specification excluding 2026 (2014–2025). The latter is not treated as a more accurate historical dataset, but as a means of assessing how strongly the latest observation influences projected demand.

8.2.3 Forecasting Horizon

The forecasting horizon covers four annual periods:

  • 2027
  • 2028
  • 2029
  • 2030

Forecasts will be reported in the original units of each target: kSU for overall HPC demand, CPU core-hours for CPU demand, and GPU-hours for GPU demand.

The GPU analysis will explicitly distinguish historical scenario-based estimates from later reported or derived values, rather than treating all historical GPU observations as equally reliable.

The resulting forecasts are conditional projections based on historical patterns and modelling assumptions. They should not be interpreted as precise estimates of future HPC requirements, particularly given uncertainty surrounding emerging GPU-intensive workloads, changes in research activity, and future infrastructure investment.

8.3 Data Preparation and Validation

The forecasting analysis uses the converted annual demand and allocation dataset produced in Chapter 05. The dataset is loaded directly from its saved RDS file to ensure that this chapter can be executed independently of the preceding analysis.

The preparation process retains the historical demand observations without modifying their original values. Validation checks confirm the expected annual coverage, uniqueness of observations, and availability of positive demand values.

8.4 Candidate Forecasting Models

8.4.1 Overall HPC Demand: Linear and Log-Linear Trends

Two initial trend models are fitted to the harmonised annual HPC demand series for 2014–2026.

The linear trend models demand directly in kSU, assuming a constant absolute change over time. The log-linear trend models the natural logarithm of demand, allowing for approximately constant proportional growth.

Both specifications are retained for subsequent evaluation. Neither is assumed to be the preferred model before out-of-sample validation.

  data = overall_model_data
)

# Log-linear trend: log(demand in kSU)
overall_loglinear <- lm(
  log(demand_ksu) ~ time,

The linear model estimates an annual absolute change in HPC demand, whereas the log-linear model estimates an approximately constant annual percentage growth rate.

The log-linear model’s fitted values must be back-transformed to kSU for comparison with observed demand. Simple exponentiation produces median predictions under a log-normal error assumption; mean predictions require appropriate bias adjustment.

Model performance and forecast accuracy will be evaluated in Chapter 08 using consistent original-scale error measures.

8.4.2 Initial Model Findings

The linear trend model estimates an average annual increase of approximately 161,992 kSU over 2014–2026. In contrast, the log-linear model estimates an annual compounded growth rate of approximately 26.3%.

Both models indicate an increasing historical demand trend. However, their assumptions differ substantially: the linear specification implies constant absolute growth, while the log-linear specification implies exponential growth.

Although both models exhibit relatively high in-sample R-squared values, these statistics are calculated on different response scales and cannot be used directly to select the preferred model.

Given the limited historical sample, heterogeneous demand estimates, and uncertainty surrounding the 2026 observation, neither model is selected at this stage. Their predictive performance will be assessed using consistent original-scale forecast errors in Chapter 08.

8.5 Sensitivity Analysis: Influence of 2026

The substantial increase in reported HPC demand in 2026 introduces uncertainty into the estimation of future demand trends.

To assess the influence of this observation, the linear and log-linear trend models are refitted using historical observations from 2014 - 2025. These results are compared with the corresponding models fitted to the complete 2014–2026 dataset.

This analysis does not assume that the 2026 observation is incorrect or should be excluded from the final forecasting model. Instead, it evaluates how sensitive the estimated trend is to the inclusion of the latest observation.

8.5.1 Trend Sensitivity Comparison

Code 
# Historical data excluding 2026
overall_pre2026_data <- overall_model_data |>
  dplyr::filter(year <= 2025)

# Refit the same model specifications
overall_linear_pre2026 <- lm(
  demand_ksu ~ time,
  data = overall_pre2026_data
)

overall_loglinear_pre2026 <- lm(
  log(demand_ksu) ~ time,
  data = overall_pre2026_data
)

# Compare trend coefficients
trend_sensitivity <- data.frame(
  model = c("Linear trend", "Log-linear trend"),

    coef(overall_linear)[["time"]],

    coef(overall_loglinear_pre2026)[["time"]]

The comparison evaluates changes in the estimated annual trend coefficients. For the linear model, the coefficient represents the annual absolute change in demand measured in kSU. For the log-linear model, the coefficient represents the annual change in log demand, which can be converted into an implied compounded growth rate.

Changes in these coefficients indicate how strongly the latest observation influences the fitted historical trend. They do not, on their own, establish whether the 2026 increase represents a structural change in underlying researcher demand.

8.5.2 Sensitivity Analysis Findings

Including the 2026 observation increases the estimated linear trend from approximately 144,357 to 161,992 kSU per year, representing a 12.2% increase in the annual absolute growth coefficient.

In contrast, the log-linear trend coefficient decreases slightly when 2026 is included, from approximately 0.239 to 0.233, equivalent to a reduction of approximately 2.4% in the estimated log-scale trend coefficient.

These results indicate that the latest observation has a greater relative influence on the estimated absolute growth rate than on the proportional growth trend.

Nevertheless, the relatively small change in the log-linear coefficient does not necessarily imply that its future forecasts are insensitive to 2026, since changes in the fitted intercept may also influence projected demand levels.

The analysis therefore supports retaining both model specifications for further evaluation. It does not establish whether the 2026 increase represents a temporary fluctuation or a sustained change in researcher demand.

8.6 CPU Demand Forecasting Models

CPU demand is modelled separately from overall HPC demand to examine the historical growth of CPU-related computing requirements.

The analysis uses the harmonised annual CPU demand estimates from 2014–2026, expressed in millions of CPU core-hours for readability. These values include directly reported and derived estimates, and therefore remain subject to the conversion assumptions documented in Chapter 05.

As with overall HPC demand, two simple trend specifications are initially considered: linear and log-linear regression.

8.6.2 CPU Trend Findings

The linear trend model estimates an average annual increase in CPU demand of approximately 81.01 million core-hours over 2014 - 2026.

The log-linear model estimates a compounded annual growth rate of approximately 18.5%, indicating substantial proportional growth in the harmonised historical CPU demand series.

Both models identify an increasing trend, although the estimated growth patterns differ. The linear model assumes constant absolute growth, whereas the log-linear model assumes approximately constant proportional growth.

The models’ in-sample R-squared values cannot be directly compared because their dependent variables are expressed on different scales. Forecast accuracy will therefore be evaluated using consistent original-scale error measures in Chapter 08.

The findings should also be interpreted cautiously because historical CPU demand includes derived estimates based on allocation shares and conversion assumptions. Consequently, the fitted trends may reflect changes in measurement and reporting practices as well as changes in underlying researcher demand.

Neither specification is selected as the preferred CPU forecasting model at this stage.

Including the 2026 observation increases the estimated linear CPU demand trend from approximately 72.41 to 81.01 million core-hours per year, representing an increase of 11.9% in the annual absolute growth coefficient.

8.7 GPU Demand Forecasting Models

GPU demand is analysed separately from overall HPC and CPU demand because GPU-hours represent a distinct resource measure with different conversion assumptions and reporting characteristics.

The harmonised GPU demand series covers 2014 - 2026. However, the historical observations are not equally suitable for forecasting.

For 2014 - 2021, GPU demand values were constructed using hypothetical conversion scenarios rather than directly reported GPU demand. These estimates are useful for exploring historical resource requirements but should not be treated as independently observed GPU demand.

For 2022 - 2026, the dataset contains a combination of reported and derived GPU demand estimates. Consequently, the later observations provide a more relevant basis for preliminary GPU forecasting, although they remain subject to reporting and conversion uncertainty.

Given these limitations, the initial GPU modelling analysis will focus on 2022–2026. Any fitted trends will be interpreted as exploratory scenarios rather than reliable estimates of long-term GPU demand growth.

8.7.3 GPU Trend Findings

The linear trend model estimates an annual increase in GPU demand of approximately 0.172 million GPU-hours over 2022–2026.

The log-linear model estimates an implied compounded annual growth rate of approximately 8.5%. However, neither trend coefficient is statistically significant at the 5% level.

Both models exhibit relatively low in-sample explanatory power, with R-squared values of approximately 0.193 and 0.174, respectively. These values are calculated on different response scales and should not be directly compared to select a model.

The historical GPU demand estimates fluctuate substantially across the five-year period. Furthermore, the series combines reported Setonix-based demand with estimates derived from GPU allocation shares and working conversion factors.

Consequently, the fitted trends provide limited evidence of a stable historical GPU demand growth pattern. The absence of a statistically significant trend should not be interpreted as evidence that future GPU demand will remain unchanged.

Given the small sample size and differences in data provenance, both models are retained only as exploratory forecasting scenarios. Further sensitivity analysis and explicit treatment of conversion uncertainty are necessary before drawing conclusions about GPU demand through 2030.

8.7.4 GPU Demand Sensitivity Analysis

The sensitivity of GPU demand trends to the 2026 observation is examined by comparing models fitted over 2022–2026 with equivalent models fitted over 2022 - 2025.

This analysis is particularly important because the recent GPU demand series contains only five annual estimates, combining reported Setonix-based demand with values derived using GPU allocation shares and conversion assumptions.

Excluding 2026 reduces the modelling sample to four observations. The resulting comparison is therefore exploratory and should not be interpreted as evidence of a structural change or as a reliable basis for selecting the preferred forecasting model.

# Forecasting Models

## Purpose

This chapter aims to develop transparent, reproducible, and statistically defensible forecasting models to estimate Australian national research supercomputing demand through 2030.

Building on the harmonised historical dataset and initial findings presented in Chapters 03 - 06, the analysis evaluates alternative approaches for modelling annual researcher demand, expressed in CPU-equivalent core-hours.

The modelling framework has four objectives:

1. Establish a suitable historical demand series for forecasting, accounting for data quality, reporting differences, and conversion assumptions.
2. Develop and compare candidate forecasting models, prioritising simple and interpretable approaches such as linear and log-linear trends.
3. Examine the sensitivity of projected demand to the substantial increase observed in 2026, distinguishing potential structural changes from temporary fluctuations.
4. Generate alternative demand projections for 2027 - 2030 to support national HPC infrastructure planning and policy discussions.

Following expert statistical feedback, particular emphasis is placed on model simplicity, transparent assumptions, and the limitations of extrapolating trends from a relatively short and heterogeneous historical dataset.

The forecasts are intended to provide evidence-informed planning scenarios rather than precise predictions of future researcher demand.

## Forecast Target and Data Suitability

### Forecast Target

The forecasting analysis considers three related but distinct measures of Australian national research supercomputing demand:

1. Overall HPC demand, expressed in thousands of service units (kSU).
2. CPU demand, expressed in CPU core-hours.
3. GPU demand, expressed in GPU-hours.

These measures represent harmonised estimates of computing resources requested by researchers, rather than allocated resources or available computing capacity. CPU and GPU demand are modelled separately because their resource units and conversion assumptions are not directly interchangeable.

The overall kSU series provides a reference for aggregate demand, while the CPU and GPU series support resource-specific forecasts
relevant to future infrastructure planning.

### Historical Data Suitability

The historical demand series provides a consistent analytical unit for forecasting, but it should not be interpreted as a fully homogeneous set of directly observed demand measurements.

Depending on the year and reporting source, historical estimates include directly reported demand, derived estimates, and proxy-based measures. Differences in facility coverage, reporting definitions, and CPU/GPU conversion assumptions introduce uncertainty into comparisons across years.

The relatively small number of annual observations also limits the complexity of forecasting models that can be estimated reliably. Consequently, simple trend-based models will be prioritised, while more flexible time-series approaches will be used as benchmarks.

The substantial increase in reported demand in 2026 presents an additional uncertainty. Available historical observations do not establish whether this increase represents a sustained shift in researcher demand, a temporary fluctuation, or a difference in measurement or reporting scope.

Accordingly, the modelling analysis will consider both the complete historical series (2014–2026) and a sensitivity specification excluding 2026 (2014–2025). The latter is not treated as a more accurate historical dataset, but as a means of assessing how strongly the latest observation influences projected demand.

### Forecasting Horizon

The forecasting horizon covers four annual periods:

- 2027
- 2028
- 2029
- 2030

Forecasts will be reported in the original units of each target: kSU for overall HPC demand, CPU core-hours for CPU demand, and GPU-hours for GPU demand.

The GPU analysis will explicitly distinguish historical scenario-based estimates from later reported or derived values, rather than treating all historical GPU observations as equally reliable.

The resulting forecasts are conditional projections based on historical patterns and modelling assumptions. They should not be interpreted as precise estimates of future HPC requirements, particularly given uncertainty surrounding emerging GPU-intensive workloads, changes in research activity, and future infrastructure investment.

## Candidate Forecasting Models

### Overall HPC Demand: Linear and Log-Linear Trends

Two initial trend models are fitted to the harmonised annual HPC demand
series for 2014–2026.

The linear trend models demand directly in kSU, assuming a constant
absolute change over time. The log-linear trend models the natural
logarithm of demand, allowing for approximately constant proportional
growth.

Both specifications are retained for subsequent evaluation. Neither
is assumed to be the preferred model before out-of-sample validation.

```{r overall-trend-models}

  dplyr::mutate(time = year - 2014)

  demand_ksu ~ time,
  data = overall_model_data
)

# Log-linear trend: log(demand in kSU)
overall_loglinear <- lm(
  log(demand_ksu) ~ time,
  data = overall_model_data
)

# Inspect fitted coefficients
summary(overall_linear)
summary(overall_loglinear)
```

The linear model estimates an annual absolute change in HPC demand, whereas the log-linear model estimates an approximately constant
annual percentage growth rate.

The log-linear model's fitted values must be back-transformed to kSU for comparison with observed demand. Simple exponentiation produces median predictions under a log-normal error assumption; mean predictions require appropriate bias adjustment.

Model performance and forecast accuracy will be evaluated in Chapter 08 using consistent original-scale error measures.

### Initial Model Findings

The linear trend model estimates an average annual increase of approximately 161,992 kSU over 2014–2026. In contrast, the log-linear
model estimates an annual compounded growth rate of approximately 26.3%.

Both models indicate an increasing historical demand trend. However, their assumptions differ substantially: the linear specification
implies constant absolute growth, while the log-linear specification implies exponential growth.

Although both models exhibit relatively high in-sample R-squared values, these statistics are calculated on different response scales
and cannot be used directly to select the preferred model.

Given the limited historical sample, heterogeneous demand estimates, and uncertainty surrounding the 2026 observation, neither model is selected at this stage. Their predictive performance will be assessed using consistent original-scale forecast errors in Chapter 08.

gpu_baseline_check <- historical_gpu_scenarios |>
  dplyr::select(
    year,
    demand_gpu_hours_baseline
  ) |>
  dplyr::left_join(
    forecast_data |>
      dplyr::select(year, demand_gpu_hours),
    by = "year"
  )

stopifnot(
  isTRUE(all.equal(
    gpu_baseline_check$demand_gpu_hours_baseline,
    gpu_baseline_check$demand_gpu_hours,
    tolerance = 1e-6
  ))
)

cat(
  "Historical GPU scenario period: 2014–2021\n",
  "Scenario observations:", nrow(historical_gpu_scenarios), "\n",
  "Baseline consistency: confirmed\n"
)

This validation confirms that the historical GPU conversion scenarios cover 2014 - 2021, preserve the expected low-to-high ordering, and reproduce the baseline GPU demand estimates contained in the converted annual dataset.

The scenarios will be retained as evidence of historical conversion uncertainty. They will not be treated as observed GPU demand when fitting the 2022 - 2026 exploratory trend models.

Any future low, baseline, and high GPU demand projections must distinguish uncertainty in the historical conversion assumptions from uncertainty in future demand growth.
