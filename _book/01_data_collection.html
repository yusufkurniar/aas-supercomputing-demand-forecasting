2.1 Purpose

The purpose of the data collection stage is to assemble a documented longitudinal evidence base for analysing Australian national research computing demand and allocation.

The project draws primarily on publicly available NCMAS outcomes and NCI and Pawsey reporting to identify historical requested compute, allocated compute, resource type, Field of Research and other available characteristics of national HPC allocations and usage (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

Additional datasets, including research funding, workforce and research activity indicators, are collected as potential structural drivers of future HPC demand where sufficiently consistent time-series data are available (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

Because the availability, scope and definition of variables differ substantially across years and sources, the purpose of this stage is not to assume that all collected observations are directly comparable. Instead, original values, units, reporting periods, source definitions and data limitations are retained so that their suitability for integration, standardisation and modelling can be assessed in subsequent stages.

2.2 Data Sources

The project uses multiple public data sources from NCI, Pawsey and NCMAS, together with research funding, workforce and Field of Research datasets.

The specific HPC source varies by analytical year. Earlier observations are primarily drawn from annual reports, allocation documents and other historical NCI and Pawsey publications, while more recent observations increasingly use direct NCMAS outcome pages, applicant guidance and facility-specific allocation information.

In several years, different documents are used for different analytical components. Allocation outcomes, requested-demand information, resource availability and Field of Research composition may therefore originate from separate official publications.

For transparency and reproducibility, the exact source document, source location and URL are retained in the underlying yearly datasets. The principal sources used for each analytical year are summarised below.

2.3 HPC Demand and Allocation Data

The main HPC dataset currently covers the period from 2013/14 to 2025/26.

The collected information includes, where available:

  • requested compute,
  • allocated compute,
  • facility,
  • HPC system,
  • resource type,
  • original compute unit,
  • Field of Research,
  • institution,
  • source, and
  • reporting period.

The level of detail varies across years because historical reporting practices and available public information are not consistent throughout the full period.

2.4 Field of Research Data

Field of Research information was collected to examine how the composition of HPC use has changed over time.

Where possible, historical FoR classifications are mapped to a consistent classification to support comparison across years.

The available FoR data primarily describes the composition of allocated compute. It should therefore not automatically be interpreted as the composition of underlying research demand.

2.5 Potential Demand Drivers

Additional datasets are being collected to investigate structural drivers of HPC demand.

These include research funding, workforce, research activity and other indicators that may be associated with changes in research compute requirements.

The suitability of these variables for forecasting will be assessed later based on their historical coverage, consistency and relationship with HPC demand.

2.6 Data Provenance

The original source, reporting period, reported unit and relevant notes are retained wherever possible.

This allows subsequent transformations and assumptions to be traced back to the original data source and helps identify observations that may not be directly comparable across years.

2.7 Data Collection Limitations

The main limitations identified during data collection include:

  • limited consistent data before 2013,
  • differences in reporting formats across years,
  • inconsistent availability of requested and allocated compute,
  • incomplete separation of CPU and GPU resources in some historical data,
  • changes in HPC systems and accounting methods, and
  • differences in FoR classifications over time.

These issues are addressed in the subsequent data integration, standardisation and conversion stages.
