11.1 Purpose and Scope

This chapter develops indicative scenarios for Australian national research high-performance computing (HPC) demand through 2030, drawing on the forecasting models evaluated in Chapter 08 and selected in Chapter 09.

The analysis considers four dimensions:

  1. Overall HPC demand, expressed in kSU.
  2. CPU demand, expressed in million CPU core-hours.
  3. GPU demand, expressed in million GPU-hours.
  4. Field of Research (FoR) allocation-share-based distributions of projected overall HPC demand.

The projection period covers 2027–2030, with 2026 representing the latest available historical observation.

The objective is to provide transparent, reproducible estimates of potential future computational requirements that can inform infrastructure planning and policy discussions.

The scenarios are conditional on the available historical data, model specifications, and stated assumptions. They should not be interpreted as definitive predictions of unconstrained national HPC demand.

11.2 Scenario Framework

The scenario framework distinguishes central statistical projections, historical sensitivity comparisons, and exploratory growth trajectories.

Demand measure Reference approach Alternative assumptions
Overall HPC Full-history linear trend Linear trend excluding 2026; log-linear growth
CPU Full-history linear trend Linear trend excluding 2026; log-linear growth
GPU Naïve statistical benchmark Linear and log-linear exploratory trajectories; sensitivity to 2026
FoR Recent-period allocation composition Latest-year and earlier-period allocation compositions

For overall HPC and CPU demand, the full-history linear trend models provide the central statistical projections because they demonstrated the strongest or most competitive historical forecasting performance.

The 2026-excluded linear specifications are retained to examine the sensitivity of projected demand to the latest observation.

Log-linear models are retained to illustrate the implications of compounded proportional growth. These projections are alternative modelling assumptions rather than statistically validated upper bounds.

For GPU demand, the naïve model serves as the statistical reference benchmark. Linear and log-linear projections are presented as exploratory trajectories because the available historical series is short and the rolling-origin evaluation contains only two test years.

FoR scenarios use historical allocation compositions to distribute projected overall HPC demand across harmonised research categories. These distributions are conditional allocation-share scenarios, not independently validated FoR-specific demand forecasts.

11.3 Interpretation of Scenario Uncertainty

Three sources of uncertainty are considered throughout the chapter:

  • Measurement uncertainty: Differences in historical reporting, resource accounting, and conversion assumptions.
  • Model uncertainty: Differences in forecasting specifications and their underlying growth assumptions.
  • Scenario uncertainty: Differences in assumed future growth and allocation composition.

The scenarios are not assigned probabilities, and the differences between model projections should not be interpreted as formal prediction intervals.

All numerical results will retain their original resource units. Overall HPC kSU, CPU core-hours, and GPU-hours will be analysed separately rather than combined without a justified conversion framework.

The analysis prioritises transparency, reproducibility, and clear communication of uncertainty over unnecessary model complexity.

11.4.1 Forecast Construction

Overall HPC demand projections are generated for 2027–2030 using the linear and log-linear models developed in Chapter 07 and evaluated in Chapter 08.

The full-history linear trend is adopted as the central statistical projection, consistent with the model-selection decision in Chapter 09.

A linear trend excluding 2026 is retained to examine sensitivity to the latest historical observation. Full-history and 2026-excluded log-linear specifications are also presented to illustrate alternative compounded-growth assumptions.

All projections are expressed in kSU and represent model-based estimates of the constructed historical demand measure, rather than forecasts of allocated computing capacity.

11.4.3 Scenario Results and Interpretation

The overall HPC demand scenarios indicate continued growth in national research computing requirements through 2030, although the projected magnitude depends substantially on the selected model specification.

Central linear projection

The selected full-history linear model projects overall HPC demand increasing from approximately 2.017 million kSU in 2027 to 2.503 million kSU in 2030.

This corresponds to an estimated annual absolute increase of approximately 161,992 kSU, consistent with the fitted historical trend.

The central projection is preferred because the linear model achieved the lowest rolling-origin RMSE and MAE among the evaluated overall HPC models in Chapter 08.

However, this result reflects historical one-step-ahead predictive performance and does not guarantee equivalent accuracy over the full projection horizon.

Sensitivity to the 2026 observation

Excluding the 2026 observation reduces the projected 2030 demand from 2,502,822 kSU to 2,285,327 kSU.

The difference is approximately 217,495 kSU, equivalent to 8.7% of the central projection.

This demonstrates that the latest observation materially influences the estimated linear growth trajectory.

The sensitivity comparison does not establish whether the 2026 increase represents a sustained change in researcher demand, a temporary development, or differences in historical reporting.

Alternative compounded-growth trajectories

The full-history log-linear model projects approximately 6.483 million kSU in 2030, while the model excluding 2026 projects approximately 6.963 million kSU.

These values are substantially higher than the central linear projection because the log-linear specifications extrapolate compounded proportional growth rather than a constant annual absolute increase.

However, Chapter 08 found that the log-linear model had substantially higher rolling-origin forecast errors than the linear model.

The log-linear projections are therefore retained as exploratory alternative growth trajectories rather than equally supported central forecasts or statistically validated upper demand bounds.

Notably, excluding 2026 increases the log-linear projection, despite reducing the corresponding linear projection. This illustrates how sensitivity to historical observations depends on the model specification.

Implications for infrastructure planning

The central statistical projection suggests that national research HPC requirements could reach approximately 2.50 million kSU by 2030 if the fitted historical linear trend continues.

The sensitivity analysis indicates that the projected magnitude depends partly on the interpretation of the latest historical observation.

The alternative log-linear trajectories demonstrate how substantially greater requirements could emerge under continued compounded growth, although the available historical evaluation provides weaker support for these specifications.

These results highlight the importance of maintaining flexibility in infrastructure planning and distinguishing projected researcher requirements from actual allocated computing capacity.

The scenarios should be interpreted as conditional, model-based estimates rather than definitive predictions of future national HPC demand.
