11  Scenario Forecasting and Capacity-Gap Analysis

11.1 Purpose

The purpose of this step is to use the selected modelling approach to forecast Australian research HPC demand to 2030 under different scenarios.

The scenarios are designed to represent different possible developments in Australian research activity and compute intensity rather than producing a single estimate of future demand.

The resulting demand forecasts are then compared with available or projected national HPC capacity to identify potential capacity gaps.

11.2 Forecast Horizon

The selected modelling approach is used to produce forecasts to 2030.

Forecast uncertainty is reported where appropriate to reflect the uncertainty associated with both the historical data and future assumptions.

11.3 Scenario Framework

Three main scenarios are considered:

  1. Status quo
  2. Restored national R&D intensity
  3. AI acceleration

Each scenario uses the same overall modelling framework but applies different assumptions about the future development of relevant demand drivers.

11.4 Status Quo Scenario

The status quo scenario represents a continuation of current research and HPC demand conditions.

Future values of the structural drivers are based on recent trends or other reasonable baseline assumptions.

This scenario provides a baseline against which the alternative scenarios can be compared.

11.5 Restored National R&D Intensity Scenario

This scenario considers higher research activity associated with an increase in Australia’s research and development intensity.

Changes in relevant drivers, such as research funding and research activity, are translated into future HPC demand using the selected modelling approach.

The assumptions used for this scenario are documented explicitly.

11.6 AI Acceleration Scenario

The AI acceleration scenario considers stronger growth in compute-intensive research associated with increasing use of artificial intelligence, machine learning and accelerated computing.

Particular attention is given to GPU demand because these workloads may have different resource requirements from traditional CPU-based research computing.

The scenario assumptions are based on available evidence and are documented separately from the historical observations.

11.7 CPU and GPU Forecasts

Where supported by the available data, CPU and GPU demand are forecast separately.

This avoids assuming that CPU core-hours and GPU hours are directly interchangeable and allows differences in their future growth to be examined.

11.8 Capacity Comparison

Forecast demand is compared with available or projected national HPC capacity where suitable capacity information is available.

Demand and capacity are only compared where their units and definitions are sufficiently consistent.

The general capacity-gap calculation is:

\[ \text{Capacity Gap}_t = \text{Forecast Demand}_t - \text{Available Capacity}_t \]

A positive value indicates forecast demand above the capacity represented in the comparison.

11.9 Facility-Equivalent Capacity

Where sufficient information is available, the estimated capacity gap may also be expressed in facility-equivalent terms.

This provides a more interpretable indication of the scale of additional computing infrastructure that may be required.

Any facility-equivalent calculation depends on assumptions about system capacity, resource type and utilisation. These assumptions are therefore reported explicitly.

11.10 Scenario Comparison

The three scenarios are compared to examine how different assumptions affect projected HPC demand to 2030.

The comparison focuses on:

  • total forecast demand
  • CPU demand
  • GPU demand
  • growth relative to the historical period
  • estimated capacity gaps
  • differences between scenarios

11.11 Uncertainty and Sensitivity

Scenario forecasts are not treated as precise predictions of future HPC demand.

Results depend on the selected model, historical data, conversion assumptions and future values assumed for the structural drivers.

Sensitivity analysis is used where appropriate to examine how changes in key assumptions affect the forecast results.

11.12 Policy Interpretation

The scenario results are interpreted in the context of Australia’s future national research computing requirements.

The analysis focuses on the potential scale and composition of future demand and the implications of different research and technology trajectories for national HPC infrastructure.

11.13 Output

The output of this step is a set of scenario forecasts to 2030 together with estimates of potential capacity gaps.

These results provide the main quantitative evidence used in the final technical report, policy summary and project presentation.
