3  Variable Definition

3.1 Purpose

The purpose of the variable definition stage is to establish clear and consistent meanings for the measures used throughout the analysis.

The variables are organised into three main groups:

  • HPC demand variables, including requested compute and related demand indicators,
  • allocation and infrastructure variables, including awarded compute and available capacity, and
  • potential structural drivers that may help explain changes in research computing demand over time.

Not all defined variables are expected to enter the final forecasting model. Some are used primarily for descriptive analysis, comparability assessment or interpretation.

Variables considered for modelling are evaluated later based on their theoretical relevance, historical coverage, consistency of measurement, temporal alignment and suitability for the relatively short annual time series available.

3.2 HPC Demand

The main outcome of interest is Australian research demand for national HPC resources.

Demand is not directly observed through a single consistent measure. The internship brief notes that national HPC allocations are supply-constrained and that researchers may also limit requests based on expected availability, meaning that observed requests may not capture the full level of underlying need (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

The analysis therefore distinguishes between requested compute, allocated compute and underlying or latent demand.

3.2.1 Requested Compute

Requested compute represents the amount of HPC resource requested by researchers through an allocation process where this information is available.

It is treated as the primary observable indicator of research demand. However, requested compute may understate underlying need if researchers limit their requests based on expected resource availability or if potential users do not submit applications (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

3.2.2 Allocated Compute

Allocated compute represents the amount of HPC resource awarded to researchers through the relevant allocation process, as reported in NCMAS outcomes and related NCI and Pawsey allocation documentation (National Computational Merit Allocation Scheme 2024, 2025, 2026).

Allocation is analysed separately from demand because the amount awarded is constrained by available resources and the allocation process.

3.2.3 Latent Demand

Latent demand refers in this project to the broader level of HPC resources that researchers might require beyond what is observed in submitted requests or awarded allocations.

This is an analytical concept rather than a directly observed variable. The internship brief highlights that observed demand may be censored by available capacity and by researchers adapting their requests to expected resource availability (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

3.3 Demand and Allocation Indicators

Several indicators are derived from the historical demand and allocation data to describe the relationship between requested and awarded compute.

The observed request-allocation gap is defined as:

\[ \text{Request-Allocation Gap}_t = \text{Requested Compute}_t - \text{Allocated Compute}_t \]

A positive value indicates that reported requests exceeded the amount of compute allocated in year (t).

The fulfilment rate is defined as:

\[ \text{Fulfilment Rate}_t = \frac{\text{Allocated Compute}_t} {\text{Requested Compute}_t} \]

A fulfilment rate below 1 indicates that allocated compute was lower than reported requested compute, while a value closer to 1 indicates that a larger proportion of reported requests was satisfied.

These indicators are used to describe allocation pressure over time. However, they are not interpreted as direct measures of total unmet research demand because requested compute may itself be constrained by expected resource availability and participation in the allocation process (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

The indicators are calculated only where requested and allocated compute refer to sufficiently comparable scope and resource definitions.

3.4 Field of Research

Field of Research (FoR) variables are used to describe the disciplinary composition of national HPC allocation.

The principal FoR variables are:

  • for_classification: the standardised research field assigned to an observation,
  • allocation_share_for: the proportion of annual allocated compute associated with each FoR,
  • allocated_compute_for: allocated compute associated with each FoR, and
  • estimated_demand_by_for_proxy: an analytical estimate of requested compute by FoR where allocation composition is applied to annual requested compute.

The FoR allocation share for field (i) in year (t) is defined as:

\[ \text{Allocation Share}_{i,t} = \frac{\text{Allocated Compute}_{i,t}} {\text{Total Allocated Compute}_{t}} \]

Where allocation composition is applied to annual requested compute, the resulting FoR-level demand measure is explicitly treated as a proxy rather than directly observed demand.

This distinction is maintained because the available FoR information primarily describes allocation composition rather than the composition of submitted requests.

3.5 Potential Structural Drivers

Potential structural drivers are candidate explanatory variables that may help describe changes in national HPC demand over time (Australian Academy of Science 2026).

They are not assumed to have a causal relationship with HPC demand and are considered for modelling only where historical coverage and measurement consistency are sufficient.

3.5.1 Research Funding

Research funding variables include available annual measures from:

  • Australian Research Council (ARC),
  • National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC), and
  • Medical Research Future Fund (MRFF).

These variables are considered as indicators of changes in the scale of funded research activity that may be associated with research computing requirements.

3.5.2 Research Workforce and Activity

Research workforce and activity variables include available measures of:

  • research workforce size, and
  • research activity.

These variables are considered as indicators of changes in the scale of the Australian research system.

3.5.3 Compute Intensity

Compute-intensity indicators describe changes in the amount or type of computing associated with research activity.

Potential indicators include:

  • compute allocation per project,
  • compute allocation by Field of Research,
  • changes in CPU allocation, and
  • changes in GPU allocation.

These measures are used primarily to investigate whether the composition and intensity of research computing are changing over time.

3.5.4 HPC Infrastructure

HPC infrastructure variables describe changes in the supply environment within which observed demand and allocation occur.

Relevant variables may include:

  • computing system,
  • resource type,
  • available compute capacity, and
  • major system commissioning or retirement.

Infrastructure variables are interpreted separately from demand because an increase in allocated compute may reflect additional available capacity rather than an equivalent increase in underlying research demand.

3.6 Variable Selection for Modelling

Not all variables defined in this chapter will be included in the final forecasting model.

Candidate variables will be assessed based on:

  • theoretical relevance to HPC demand,
  • historical data coverage,
  • consistency of measurement,
  • temporal alignment with the demand series,
  • observed relationship with the selected demand measure,
  • availability or defensible assumptions over the forecast horizon, and
  • the limited number of annual observations available for modelling.

Given the short annual time series, the final modelling approach will prioritise a small number of interpretable variables rather than a large set of predictors. Variables that are useful for descriptive analysis or interpretation may therefore be retained without being included in the final forecasting model.
