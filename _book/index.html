1 Introduction

1.1 Project Background

High-performance computing (HPC) is an important component of Australia’s research infrastructure (Australian Academy of Science 2024), supporting computationally intensive research across a wide range of scientific fields.

Australia has two national Tier-1 HPC facilities accessible to researchers: the National Computational Infrastructure (NCI) and the Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre (National Computational Infrastructure, n.d.; Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre, n.d.). Several Tier-2 HPC facilities also operate across Australian research institutions.

As research becomes increasingly data and compute-intensive, understanding how demand for HPC resources is changing is important for planning Australia’s future national research infrastructure.

1.2 Project Purpose

The purpose of this project is to develop a robust forecast of Australian research demand for high-performance computing to 2030.

The forecast is intended to support national policy discussions around public compute investment and future research infrastructure planning.

The project combines data engineering, descriptive analysis and forecasting using historical Australian HPC data and other indicators that may help explain changes in research compute demand.

1.3 The Forecasting Problem

One of the main challenges is that observed HPC allocation does not necessarily represent actual research demand.

National allocation schemes operate under limited available capacity. As a result, allocated compute represents the amount of resource awarded rather than necessarily the amount researchers required.

Requested compute provides additional information about demand, but may also underestimate actual need if researchers limit their requests based on expected availability or if potential users do not apply.

The project therefore distinguishes between:

  • requested compute,
  • allocated compute,
  • the observed gap between requested and allocated compute, and
  • underlying or latent demand.

1.4 Project Objectives

The project aims to:

  1. build a documented longitudinal dataset of Australian HPC activity,
  2. improve comparability of compute measures across years, facilities and hardware generations,
  3. distinguish CPU and GPU compute where possible,
  4. examine historical demand, allocation and oversubscription,
  5. analyse changes in Field of Research composition,
  6. investigate potential structural drivers of HPC demand,
  7. develop and compare suitable forecasting models,
  8. forecast HPC demand to 2030 under different scenarios, and
  9. estimate potential future gaps between demand and available capacity.

1.5 Analysis Framework

The analysis follows ten main steps:

  1. Data collection
  2. Potential variable definition
  3. Data integration
  4. Data standardisation
  5. Data conversion
  6. Initial analysis and data manipulation
  7. Model development and benchmarking
  8. Model evaluation
  9. Model selection
  10. Scenario forecasting and capacity-gap analysis
